# "The Bat Bomb That Burned Its Own Base" - script v3 (Deutsch)

Format: vertical short, German (Deutsch), about 3.1 words/s with pauses squeezed to 0.12 s (voice still open: Kokoro has no German, e.g. Piper `de_DE` or ElevenLabs), one narrator who talks as "I" and comments, hook on the first frame, no intro or outro.
Second short of the "animals in war" series after the pigeons.

## Facts and sources
- Lytle S. Adams, a dental surgeon from Irwin, Pennsylvania, visited Carlsbad Caverns, saw the bats fly out, and heard about Pearl Harbor on the car radio on the way home (7 December 1941). (Task & Purpose; Air & Space Forces)
- He knew bats roost under roofs before dawn and that Japanese cities were largely built of wood. (Wikipedia)
- January 1942: letter to the White House. Roosevelt passed it on with a note: "This man is not a nut. It sounds like a perfectly wild idea but is worth looking into." (Wikipedia, quoting Couffer)
- The project went to the Army Air Forces; Adams assembled the team. (Wikipedia)
- Mexican free-tailed bat, about 14 g (half an ounce), carried an incendiary of 15-18 g. (Wikipedia)
- Bats were cooled into hibernation in ice cube trays and refrigerators. (Wikipedia; Air & Space Forces)
- Carrier: a 1.5 m tube with 26 trays holding 1,040 bats, opened by parachute at about 1,200 m. (Wikipedia)
- 15 May 1943, Carlsbad Army Airfield Auxiliary Air Base, New Mexico: armed bats got loose (the heat woke them / a door left open), roosted under buildings and set the new base on fire. (Wikipedia; Task & Purpose)
- They set fire to a hangar and a general's car. (Air & Space Forces)
- Captain Carr's report only said testing ended "when a fire destroyed a large portion of the test material." (Air & Space Forces)
- Navy (Project X-Ray) from August 1943, then the Marines. Cancelled in 1944 by Admiral King because it would not be ready before mid-1945; about $2 million spent; never used in combat. (Wikipedia; Air & Space Forces)

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat_bomb, https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/1090bat/, https://taskandpurpose.com/history/bat-bombs-burned-base/
- [ ] Key facts checked against a primary source: Jack Couffer, "Bat Bomb: World War II's Other Secret Weapon" (1992, he was on the team), or Captain Carr's report of 8 June 1943.

## English version (v2, for a later English upload)
America strapped tiny firebombs to bats, and the first thing they burned was American.
I'm serious, in 1941 a dentist named Lytle watches millions of bats pour out of a cave.
On the drive home the radio says Pearl Harbor, and Lytle remembers that Japanese houses are made of wood.
And bats, he knows, love to sleep under roofs.
So a dentist writes to the President, and the President passes it on: "This man is not a nut."
Honestly, I would frame that note and hang it in my office.
Every bat gets a firebomb heavier than itself, and they nap in ice cube trays in a fridge.
Wait, what if the bats wake up too early?
In 1943, on a hot day at a brand new air base, they do.
They fly under the nearest roofs, and the hangar and a general's car go up in flames.
You see, a bat doesn't pick a target, it picks the closest dark corner.
Ever brought a new cat home? It's under the nearest bed in seconds.
The report just says: "A fire destroyed a large portion of the test material."
Test material, that's a funny way to say "our own base."
The bat bomb never reached Japan. It only ever beat America.
